We homeschool. Here is all of it.

Most homeschool advice is somebody telling you it worked for them. This is the whole operation instead — what we actually do each day, how many hours it really takes, and what an outside examiner says about it. Including the parts that don’t look good.

Are we doing enough hours? The question every homeschooling parent asks at 9pm and can never quite answer. Put your week in, see it against the federal figures for US public schools, and find the subject that quietly stopped happening. Free · runs in your browser · collects nothing

The number that changed how we see our day

10.3

hours a week of a US public elementary school week are not core instruction — about a third of it.

Transitions, attendance, lining up, assemblies, and the ordinary friction of moving twenty-five children around a building. It isn’t anyone’s fault and it isn’t wasted exactly. But it is why a homeschool day that feels suspiciously short can cover the same ground.

If you have ever finished at lunchtime and wondered whether you were shortchanging your kid — that number is for you. The full working is here, sourced to the federal survey.

Who is actually doing this

A single father with full custody, a daughter in upper elementary, and an accredited curriculum that grades her work so we can’t grade it ourselves.

The two things people ask about homeschooling are whether the child is isolated and whether the parent is qualified to teach. Both are fair questions. We answer both with specifics on the who we are page — her actual week, what she is like to teach, and what the parent running this did before.

A yearly outside test

Iowa Form E, norm-referenced on 2024 data, starting next enrolment year. A percentile against US students generally, which a report card cannot give you. Why Iowa, and why not yet.

The measures were fixed in advance. We wrote down what we would report before any of it existed, so there is nothing to quietly leave out later. When a score is bad it goes up anyway, with the reason beside it.

Two already on the site. A timed reading quiz came back ten points below the same child’s result a week earlier, at an identical reading speed — she was tired, and that sits next to the number. And Writing has declined three years running, 83 to 80 to 79, which is the first thing on the results page rather than the last.

Three years of graded results, in full Every subject, every year, marked by the accrediting body — the strong ones, the flat ones, and the one that has been sliding since Grade 1. Plus: the standardized test we are adding, and why

What we don’t claim

We are not telling you homeschooling beats school. One child, no control group. The research does not settle it in either direction — and that includes the leading pro-homeschooling research body, which states plainly that the studies so far do not show homeschooling causes the results they report.

Anyone selling you certainty on that is selling you something. The useful question is not whether homeschooling is better in the abstract. It is whether you can run the thing — and that one has an answer, which is what this site is for.
