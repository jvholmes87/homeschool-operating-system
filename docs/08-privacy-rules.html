08 — Privacy rules for this repository

Binding on every file here and on anything published from it. The student in this programme is a minor. These rules are not editorial preference.

The student is never identified

  • No name. Not full, not first, not initials. She is "the student".
  • No age or date of birth. "Upper elementary" is the limit.
  • No photographs, of her or of her work in her handwriting.
  • No school-issued identifiers — account numbers, student IDs, enrolment numbers.

Her grades and hours are published. Her identity is not. The practical effect is that her record never surfaces in a search for her name, which is the part that follows her into adulthood.

This is thin cover, and it is meant to be. The repository author is named, and anyone who knows him can work out who the student is. The rule is not anonymity against someone who already knows her; it is that nothing here is indexed against her name.

Locations and schedules are never publishable

A public document that says a named-or-guessable ten-year-old is at a specific place at a specific time is a safety problem, however ordinary the activity.

Never publish Publish instead
Named organisations, troop or unit numbers the category: "scouting", "a sports club"
Days and times of any activity hours per week
Towns, bases, facilities, tutoring centres nothing, or the country at most
Anything that fixes her to a place and a time a weekly total

"Scouting, one meeting per week, 1.5–2 hrs" makes exactly the same argument as naming the troop and the night, and tells a stranger nothing useful.

Curriculum content

No publisher's content is reproduced here: no word lists, quiz items, answer keys, grading tables or lesson text. Naming a curriculum you use is factual and fine. Reproducing it is not. The two are easy to confuse under deadline — when in doubt, describe the structure and omit the content.

Tools published from this repository

Anything interactive — the coverage benchmark, any calculator — collects nothing and sends nothing. No analytics, no form posts, no third-party scripts, no storage. A family checking whether they do enough hours should not have to trust anyone with the answer.

If that ever changes, it needs a written data policy first, and it must not be bundled quietly into a release.

Before any commit

  1. Does any file name, date-of-birth, photograph or school identifier appear?
  2. Does any file fix a child to a place and a time?
  3. Is any publisher content reproduced rather than described?
  4. Does any new interactive page call out to the network?

The repository's leak scan covers (1) and (3) mechanically. (2) and (4) need a human.
