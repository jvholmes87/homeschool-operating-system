08 — Privacy rules for this repository

Binding on every file here and on anything published from it. The student in our homeschool is a minor. These rules are not editorial preference.

The student is never identified

  • No name. Not full, not first, not initials. She is "the student".
  • No age or date of birth. "Upper elementary" is the limit — not the year, not the birthday, not "she turns ten this month".
  • No photographs, of her or of her work in her handwriting.
  • No school-issued identifiers — account numbers, student IDs, enrollment numbers.

Her grades and hours are published. Her identity is not. The practical effect is that her record never surfaces in a search for her name, which is the part that follows her into adulthood.

This is thin cover, and it is meant to be. The repository author is named, and anyone who knows him can work out who the student is. The rule is not anonymity against someone who already knows her; it is that nothing here is indexed against her name.

Schedules are never publishable. Region is.

What locates a child is not a place on its own. It is a place plus a time plus a way to recognize her. Any two of those three are inert; all three together are the safety problem, however ordinary the activity.

So the rule is drawn around the combination, not around the word "location":

Never publish Publish instead
Days, times, or recurring schedule of any activity hours per week
Named organizations, troop or unit numbers the category: "scouting", "a Korean sports club"
Addresses, specific facilities, the academy or club she attends the region, and the category of place
Her name or photograph alongside any of the above neither, ever

Region is published deliberately — the country, and the installation whose community this family is part of. That was a considered change, not a slip. The program's entire shape is a consequence of being an overseas military family: the ship-by-mail deadlines, the time-zone gap with the school, the hagwon in the middle of a school week, the base scouting and youth-sports infrastructure doing the socialisation work. Strip the region out and the case study stops being useful to the families most likely to need it, who are disproportionately military and overseas.

What that costs is accepted honestly: it narrows the pool. What it does not do is produce a findable child, because the schedule and the name — the other two legs — are absent and stay absent.

"Scouting, one meeting per week, 1.5–2 hrs, on base" makes exactly the same argument as naming the troop and the night, and tells a stranger nothing actionable.

Date of birth

Her date of birth is never published, which is a change worth stating explicitly because birthdays are the detail families leak most casually — a cake photo, a "she turns ten today" post. DOB plus region plus a surname is enough to start matching records. It lives in the private operating files only, where it is needed to set the school calendar.

Curriculum content

No publisher's content is reproduced here: no word lists, quiz items, answer keys, grading tables or lesson text. Naming a curriculum you use is factual and fine. Reproducing it is not. The two are easy to confuse under deadline — when in doubt, describe the structure and omit the content.

Tools published from this repository

Anything interactive — the coverage benchmark, any calculator — collects nothing and sends nothing. No analytics, no form posts, no third-party scripts, no storage. A family checking whether they do enough hours should not have to trust anyone with the answer.

If that ever changes, it needs a written data policy first, and it must not be bundled quietly into a release.

Before any commit

  1. Does any file carry her name, date of birth, photograph or a school identifier?
  2. Does any file put a child at a place on a given day or at a given time?
  3. Does any file name the troop, academy or club?
  4. Is any publisher content reproduced rather than described?
  5. Does any new interactive page call out to the network?

The leak scan covers (1) and (4) mechanically — the docs build exits non-zero if her name appears in generated output, so a slip fails the publish rather than shipping. (2), (3) and (5) need a human.

Part of an open operating model for accredited homeschooling — free to copy and adapt, source on GitHub.

The student in our homeschool is never identified — see the privacy rules. No analytics, no cookies, no trackers, no third-party scripts.
