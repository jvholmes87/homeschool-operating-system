05 — Metrics that are tracked but never graded

The principle

Some measures are worth watching and must never affect a score. Reading rate is the canonical example: it diagnoses, it does not assess.

Mixing them corrupts both. A student who knows speed is graded reads faster and comprehends less — trading away the thing that is graded for the thing that is merely measured.

Implementation

  1. Physical separation in the sheet. The metric lives in columns outside the graded range. No average can reach it even by accident.
  2. Stated on the student's own sheet, in plain words: your reading speed is not part of your grade, we just keep track of it.
  3. Stated on the supervisor sheet, so nobody helpfully deducts for a slow time.
  4. Two typed inputs only — word count and elapsed time. Everything else is a formula. Arithmetic done by hand is arithmetic done wrong eventually.

Banding

Bands are anchored to the publisher's own printed benchmarks, not invented:

Band Source
Floor the baseline printed on the student's own progress graph
Target the rate the teacher's notes allow for the grade level
Ceiling the top of that graph's scale

If you cannot point at where a threshold came from, you have invented a standard and attached authority to it.

Flags must not claim causation

A flag combining two signals — high rate, low score — is useful. The wording is where it goes wrong.

Too fast — comprehension slipping asserts a cause. The first two real data points destroyed it: 175 wpm scored 90, 177 wpm scored 80. Rate moved 2; score moved 10. The student was tired, which the numbers cannot show.

The flag now reads Check — fast pace, N missed. Same trigger, no claim, and a Note column beside it for the condition.

A metric without its context becomes misleading the moment everyone forgets. Budget a free-text column next to every automated indicator. It is the cheapest thing in the system and the first thing omitted.

Trend before level

A single reading is noise. The sheet shows the change since the previous one, because the expectation is improvement over a year, not a number on a day.
