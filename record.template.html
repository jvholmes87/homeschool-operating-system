Three years, every subject

The record so far

Three consecutive years of accredited enrollment, published in full. Every number below was marked by the accrediting body, not by the parent — which is the only reason it is worth anything to you.

Final grades, Grades 1 to 3

One panel per subject on a shared scale, so the comparison is honest. A subject with a shorter line was not taught in the earlier years — Spelling begins in Grade 2, History and Science in Grade 3.

The same figures as a table

Letter grades use the accrediting body’s 10-point scale (A+ 98–100 · A 93–97 · A− 90–92 · B+ 87–89 · B 83–86 · B− 80–82 · C+ 77–79). Chart and table are generated from one list of numbers, so they cannot drift apart.

What this actually shows

Writing has gone down three years running: 83, 80, 79. It is the lowest subject on the board and the only one outside the A–B range. We said on the front page that a bad number goes up anyway, so here it is, first, before the good ones.

Writing in this curriculum is penmanship, and it is graded against sample papers rather than a rubric — there is no list of criteria to work through, only exemplars to compare against. That makes it the easiest subject to let slide, because nothing fails loudly. Grade 4 introduces cursive, which is either the fix or the thing that makes it worse, and either way it will be on this page next year.

The rest of it, plainly

  • Arithmetic has been the strongest subject throughout — 96, 99, 96. Consistent with her working above grade level, and the reason that subject is handed to a specialist three days a week rather than taught at our table.
  • Bible climbed hardest: 87 to 98. It also holds the single worst mark in the whole record — a C, 75, in the very first grading period of Grade 1. That is what the start of an untested program looks like, and it is worth leaving visible.
  • Reading rose then flattened: 86, 90, 90. Flat at an A− is a fine place to sit, and it is why reading speed is now tracked separately — a grade that stops moving tells you nothing about whether the child is still improving. How that is measured without grading it.
  • Language slipped from 96 to 90. Still an A−, so it is not a crisis, but it is the second subject pointing downward and it is being watched rather than explained away.

And the caveats, which matter more than the grades

These are one child’s grades. No control group, no comparison student, nothing that generalises. They cannot tell you homeschooling works, and they are not offered as evidence that it does — see what we do and don’t claim.

Grades are not a norm-referenced score. A 96 in Arithmetic means she met this curriculum’s standard for her grade level. It does not say where she stands against students nationally, and anyone presenting a report card as though it did is overselling. That gap is exactly what the next section is about.

The pace just changed, and that is unproven. Grades 1 through 3 each ran roughly a full calendar year. Grade 4 is being run in about six months — the floor the accrediting body allows. Whether the results hold up at that pace is an open question, and this page is where the answer will show up whichever way it goes.

Next year: a standardized test, and why

Everything above has the same weakness. It is criterion-referenced — it says she met this curriculum’s standard. It cannot say how she compares to children her age across the country, because it was never designed to.

That is the gap a sceptical parent, a university admissions office, or a school registrar actually cares about. So we are closing it: she will sit the Iowa Assessments (Form E) during the next enrollment year, and the percentiles will be published on this page beside the grades.

Why Iowa, specifically

The norms are current

Form E is normed on 2024 data, weighted to federal enrollment figures. The main alternative’s norms date to 2018 and were re-normed on a sample with a heavier share of private schools — which flatters a homeschooler’s percentile for no good reason.

It is nationally norm-referenced

It answers the question grades can’t: not “did she meet the standard” but “where does she sit against US students generally.” That is the number that travels.

Third party, start to finish

Not written, administered or scored by the parent. The whole point of this site is that the parent’s opinion of the child’s work is the least interesting evidence available.

It costs nothing

Accredited enrollment includes one test per enrollment year at no charge. If you are accredited and not using it, you are leaving the single most credible number available to you on the table.

Why next year and not now

Two reasons, and neither is stalling. The allowance is one test per enrollment year, so spending it in the middle of a grading period wastes it — it is worth more once a full grade level has been completed under the current program. And a baseline taken at the start of an accelerated pace, before the pace has been proven, measures the old program rather than this one.

Booking requires about 30 days’ notice. That call is already on the calendar rather than in somebody’s memory, which is roughly the whole philosophy of this site in one sentence.

What we will do with a bad result. Publish it here, in the same place and the same format as a good one, with the reason beside it. A measure you only report when it flatters you is not a measure — it is marketing. The Writing column above is the evidence that this is how it actually goes.
